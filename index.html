About

I research human–AI interaction in clinical settings. In controlled studies with radiologists, I use eye-tracking and physiological measures to show how AI explainability and uncertainty, and clinical pressures such as stress and fatigue, shape visual attention, trust and decision-making.

With a background in computer science and software engineering, I work across the full stack: building multimodal data-acquisition platforms that integrate eye-tracking and wearable sensors, designing clinical AI interfaces, and running user studies end to end.

What drives me is the gap between how systems are built and how experts actually use them under real conditions. I treat that gap as both a research problem and a design problem.

Experimental studies

How AI changes radiologists' reading

Conducting controlled experiments with radiologists, on how AI assistance, explanation methods, reporting UI, decision-support format and fatigue affect diagnostic performance, gaze and reasoning in chest radiography and breast MRI.

  • Experimental design
  • UX Research & Eye-tracking
  • Mixed-effects models

Multimodal sensing

Measuring fatigue, stress and cognitive load

Synchronized acquisition setups that combine medical reading interfaces with eye-tracking (Tobii, Pupil Labs) and wearable sensors (Empatica), plus the pipelines that turn them into digital biomarkers.
